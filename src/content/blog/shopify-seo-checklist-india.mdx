Getting a Shopify store online is only the first step. The harder job is helping the right customer find the right product and feel confident enough to buy it.

For Indian eCommerce brands, that journey often happens on a mobile phone and may include Google Search, Google Images, Shopping results, WhatsApp, marketplace comparisons and a final check of delivery or return terms.

This checklist focuses on the work that improves those journeys. It avoids shortcuts and concentrates on product clarity, store structure, merchant data, performance and trust.

<div class="tldr" data-speakable>
**A search-friendly Shopify store makes its products easy to discover, understand and buy. Start with accurate product data, useful collection pages, clear shipping and return information, strong internal links and a fast mobile experience.**
</div>

## 1. Connect the correct domain

Use one secure production domain as the main address for the store. Confirm that:

- HTTP redirects to HTTPS
- alternate Shopify domains redirect to the preferred domain
- both `www` and non-`www` versions resolve consistently
- internal links use the preferred address
- the canonical URL on important pages matches the intended public URL

A domain migration or redesign should include a redirect plan for every valuable old product, collection and content URL. Sending all removed pages to the homepage is rarely helpful. Redirect each old URL to the closest relevant replacement.

## 2. Submit the Shopify sitemap to Search Console

Shopify automatically creates a sitemap at `/sitemap.xml`. Add the preferred domain to Google Search Console and submit that sitemap.

Use Search Console to monitor:

- indexed product and collection pages
- pages excluded because of redirects, canonical choices or `noindex`
- crawl errors
- search queries and landing pages
- mobile usability and Core Web Vitals patterns

Do not edit `robots.txt.liquid` simply because an indexing report looks unfamiliar. Shopify states that its default robots file is designed for store SEO. Change it only when you understand which crawl behaviour needs to change.

[Read Shopify’s robots.txt guidance](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/promoting-marketing/seo/editing-robots-txt).

## 3. Build collections around buying intent

Collections are often stronger search landing pages than individual products because they answer a broader need.

Useful collection themes can include:

- product type
- material or ingredient
- use case
- recipient
- occasion or festival
- size or fit
- price range
- location-specific delivery, when genuinely available

Choose names customers recognise. A creative internal campaign name may suit advertising but may not explain the products to someone arriving from Google.

Every important collection should have a clear heading, a short introduction and enough supporting information to help the shopper choose. Avoid filling the page with repetitive keywords.

## 4. Write product titles for people first

A useful product title says what the item is and adds the detail that differentiates it.

For example, “Blue Cotton Kurta with Hand Block Print” is more informative than a collection code or a poetic name used alone.

Keep titles consistent across:

- the product page
- Merchant Center
- social catalogues
- marketplace exports
- advertising feeds

Do not add words such as “best,” “cheap” or “No. 1” unless the page genuinely supports the claim.

## 5. Replace thin or copied descriptions

Product descriptions should help customers make a decision. Include the information relevant to the product, such as:

- material, ingredients or composition
- dimensions, weight or quantity
- fit and sizing
- colour and finish
- care instructions
- what is included
- intended use
- country of origin where applicable
- warranty or shelf-life information where applicable
- dispatch and delivery expectations

Put important information in readable page text. Do not place size charts, ingredients or care instructions only inside images.

If several variants share one page, make the differences clear. Customers should not have to guess whether changing a variant affects price, availability, size or delivery.

## 6. Optimise page titles and search descriptions

Write a distinct SEO title and meta description for important collections, products and editorial pages.

A practical pattern for a collection page is:

`Primary collection or product benefit | Brand`

The description should explain what is available and give a genuine reason to visit. Adding “India” to every title is not necessary. Use geographic language only when it improves meaning, such as pan-India delivery, products made in India or a location-specific service.

Google may rewrite titles and descriptions when another version better matches a search. Clear page content still matters more than trying to control every displayed word.

## 7. Keep URLs short and stable

Use readable Shopify handles and avoid changing them after a page begins receiving traffic or links.

When a URL must change:

1. Create a permanent redirect from the old URL.
2. Update menus and internal links.
3. Update campaign, feed and profile links.
4. Confirm the new page has the correct canonical URL.
5. Test the old address after publishing.

Avoid creating multiple near-identical pages only to target small keyword variations.

## 8. Improve internal links

Google uses links between pages to understand site structure and relative importance. Shoppers use the same links to find the next useful product.

Link priority collections and products from relevant places, including:

- the main navigation
- the homepage
- collection introductions
- buying guides
- related-product sections
- product-care articles
- gift and festival guides

Use link text that describes the destination. “Shop cotton kurtas” is more useful than several identical “click here” links.

[See Google’s eCommerce site-structure guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/help-google-understand-your-ecommerce-site-structure).

## 9. Control filters and duplicate paths

Filters improve shopping, but unlimited combinations can produce many low-value URLs. Review URLs created by tags, sorting, filters, internal search and third-party apps.

Ask three questions:

- Does this URL offer a useful and distinct product selection?
- Can Google reach it through normal links?
- Should it appear as a search result?

Keep valuable collection pages indexable. Consolidate or prevent indexing of paths that merely repeat the same products in a different order. Do not block a URL in `robots.txt` when Google needs to crawl it to see a canonical or `noindex` instruction.

## 10. Check product structured data

Shopify themes and apps commonly generate product structured data, but it still needs validation.

For products available to buy, Google’s merchant listing guidance supports information such as:

- product name and images
- price and INR currency
- availability
- SKU, GTIN or MPN where applicable
- brand
- condition
- ratings based on genuine reviews
- shipping details
- return policy information

Test representative products with Google’s Rich Results Test and review the Merchant listings and Product snippets reports in Search Console.

Never add fabricated ratings or reviews. Structured data must agree with the information visible to customers.

[Review Google’s Product structured-data guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product).

## 11. Set up Merchant Center carefully

Google recommends using both on-page product structured data and a Merchant Center feed to maximise eligibility for shopping experiences.

For an Indian store, confirm that the feed and storefront agree on:

- product ID
- title and description
- product URL
- image URL
- INR price
- availability
- condition
- brand and product identifiers
- shipping cost and delivery coverage
- return information

Google’s product-data specification lists shipping costs as required for Shopping ads and free listings in India. Check current Merchant Center requirements before launch because requirements can change.

[Check the Google Merchant Center product-data specification](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/7052112?hl=en).

## 12. Make delivery and returns easy to find

Indian shoppers often want answers before checkout:

- Is Cash on Delivery available?
- Which PIN codes are serviceable?
- How long will dispatch take?
- Is shipping free above a certain order value?
- Can the product be returned or exchanged?
- Who pays return shipping?
- What happens when an item arrives damaged?

Publish honest policies and link them from product pages, the footer and checkout journey. Keep the same information in Merchant Center and other sales channels.

Clear policies support SEO indirectly by improving trust and reducing avoidable exits. They can also be represented in supported merchant structured data.

## 13. Optimise images without making them look poor

Strong product photography helps both conversion and visibility across Google Images and Lens.

For each priority product:

- use sharp, well-lit images
- show useful angles and scale
- avoid putting every detail inside one poster graphic
- write concise alt text that describes the image
- use sensible dimensions and modern formats
- prevent oversized files from slowing mobile pages
- keep the primary image consistent with product feeds

Compression should remove unnecessary file weight, not visible product detail.

## 14. Improve the mobile shopping experience

Most problems are easier to notice on a real phone than in a desktop editor.

Test the complete journey on common mobile widths:

- navigation
- collection filters
- product gallery
- variant selection
- Add to Cart
- cart updates
- discount codes
- address entry
- payment
- order confirmation

Watch for intrusive popups, shifting layouts, sticky elements covering buttons, slow app scripts and controls that are too small to use comfortably.

Performance matters, but a fast page that makes buying confusing is not a successful page.

## 15. Make the storefront accessible

Accessibility improves the experience for customers using keyboards, screen readers, zoom, voice control or other assistive technology.

Check that:

- headings follow a meaningful order
- menus and dialogs work with a keyboard
- form fields have clear labels
- colour is not the only way information is communicated
- focus is visible
- error messages explain how to correct a problem
- product options have understandable names
- images have appropriate alt text
- text and controls have sufficient contrast

Start with our [web accessibility and ADA compliance hub](/blog/accessibility/web-accessibility-ada-compliance-hub/) and [ADA and WCAG compliance service](/services/ada-compliant-web-design/).

## 16. Publish content that supports products

Create content when it helps customers choose, use or care for a product. Useful formats include:

- buying guides
- size and fit guides
- ingredient explainers
- comparison pages
- styling ideas
- installation instructions
- maintenance and care guides
- festival gifting guides
- answers to pre-purchase questions

Connect each article to relevant products and collections. A blog that receives traffic but never helps a customer continue shopping is a missed opportunity.

## 17. Earn trust beyond the store

Search visibility does not depend on adding the store to hundreds of directories.

Build genuine signals through:

- complete business and contact information
- consistent brand profiles
- useful press coverage
- partnerships and stockist pages
- expert contributions
- authentic customer reviews
- strong customer support

Avoid buying bulk links or publishing the same promotional article across low-quality websites.

## 18. Track revenue, not only rankings

Review performance monthly using Search Console, Merchant Center and analytics.

Track:

- non-branded search clicks
- product and collection landing pages
- Merchant listing errors
- queries with strong impressions but weak clicks
- organic add-to-cart and checkout activity
- organic revenue
- mobile conversion problems
- products receiving impressions but no sales

Prioritise improvements by business value. A collection ranking in position six for a commercial query may deserve attention before a blog post that already ranks first but does not lead to sales.

## A practical monthly Shopify SEO routine

### Every week

- review Search Console indexing alerts
- fix Merchant Center product disapprovals
- check important products for price and availability mismatches
- test one mobile shopping journey

### Every month

- compare organic landing pages and revenue
- improve one priority collection
- refresh thin product descriptions
- review internal links to best sellers
- test structured data on representative products
- check new app scripts and performance changes

### Every quarter

- audit redirects and broken links
- review filters and duplicate URLs
- reassess navigation and collection structure
- audit accessibility across the full purchase journey
- compare Merchant Center, storefront and analytics data

## Final checklist

- [ ] Preferred domain and HTTPS are consistent
- [ ] `/sitemap.xml` is submitted in Search Console
- [ ] Priority products and collections are indexable
- [ ] Page titles and descriptions are distinct and useful
- [ ] Product copy answers real buying questions
- [ ] Variants, prices and stock information are accurate
- [ ] Important pages receive internal links
- [ ] Product structured data validates
- [ ] Merchant Center data matches the storefront
- [ ] INR pricing, shipping and returns are clear
- [ ] Product images are sharp and efficiently delivered
- [ ] Mobile purchase journeys work smoothly
- [ ] Storefront controls are accessible
- [ ] Search performance is tied to enquiries and revenue
